Minerva / 01

Role / Mechanical team lead

Designing the ground segment and the payload shell.

Minerva is a reusable, low-cost platform for testing CubeSat payloads before orbital launch. I designed the Ground Station Receiving Unit from concept through PDR, integrating antennas, radios, sensors, and electronics into a mission-tested communications device.

The work in context

From launch day
back to the model.

Every design decision on Minerva has to survive a practical loop: build it, integrate it, fly it, and use the evidence to improve the next iteration.

Second high-altitude campaign
Ground station receiving unit

Concept, integration, hardware.

The GSRU moved through CAD, 3D printing, electronics integration, and hardware testing. The enclosure had to protect the system while leaving the antennas, controls, and field workflow accessible.

Payload shell evolution

V1 → V2 → V3

The shell evolved from a first prototype into a lighter, scalable architecture. The current direction uses struts instead of bulky walls and supports a path from 1U to 3U configurations.

Verification

Analysis before flight.

I performed preliminary thermal and static structural simulations for a 2 kg payload at 35 km, including a −36.1°C ambient condition and 23.4 N upward lift loading at the attachment points.

Zephyr / 02

Role / Structures and composites analyst

Closing the gap between equations and the shop floor.

For a Mach 1.2 sounding rocket, I modeled shear, normal, and von Mises stresses under combined loading. Sensitivity studies informed a fin redesign and center-of-pressure shift. I then supported CNC machining and composite fabrication for the hardware those calculations described.

60%reduction in manual evaluation time through MATLAB section-analysis tools
Research / 03

Role / Researcher and presenter

Turning models into decisions.

I built and presented computational models for gravitational fields and aeroelastic flutter. The work sharpened how I translate coupled differential equations, mass and stiffness matrices, and eigenvalues into engineering conclusions that another person can inspect.
